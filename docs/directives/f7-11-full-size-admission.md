## Lead decision: the next admission gate
1. Admit render targets up to the current display mode (and Quake III's 640x480/800x600/1024x768) with D16, inside the claimed 3D heap, when the boot probe qualified the chip AND a new full-size qualification step passed on this boot: render a deterministic full-size scene (both stages, depth, blend, mip/bilinear) into the heap, read back sampled rows/columns and checksum them against the scalar reference within the contract tolerances, verify guard pages untouched and restore; record `[3d] fullsize result=qualified|refused ...` with evidence. Without it, stay at the 64x64 admission (software for real games).
2. Raise the per-submission bounds to useful sizes (Quake III frames: hundreds of triangles per batch) with validated packet lengths and FIFO/BCI back-pressure (the contract's bounded execution rules), per-batch fences, and the watchdog/quarantine unchanged.
3. Varying projective texture sampling: qualify native perspective-correct sampling against the scalar reference for Quake III's ranges (the f7-09 precision rejection may be relaxed only where the new tests prove equivalence within tolerance).
4. Keep every T23-dependent step behind the boot probe and the new full-size step; QEMU (no Savage) must stay exactly on the software path.
5. Host tests with the fake Savage engine: full-size qualification pass/fail paths, large batches, back-pressure, guard violation, perspective sampling equivalence, Quake-like replay at 640x480 through the hardware path matching the software golden; a performance model of submission cost per Quake III frame on the Pentium III.
